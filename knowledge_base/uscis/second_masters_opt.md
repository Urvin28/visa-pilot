# OPT after a second master's degree

Whether someone can receive post-completion OPT after a second master's depends mainly on the person's prior OPT use at the master's educational level, plus the current degree and F-1 eligibility requirements. A second master's does not automatically create a fresh 12-month OPT allowance.

## General rule

Federal regulations allow up to 12 months of practical training and provide eligibility for another 12 months when a student advances to a higher educational level. A master's degree is one educational level. Therefore:

- **No prior post-completion OPT used at the master's level:** The student may have OPT eligibility remaining after finishing a second master's, if all other requirements are satisfied. A second master's by itself is not a new higher educational level, so it does not independently reset the 12-month allowance.
- **Some master's-level OPT already used:** The remaining amount, if any, may be limited by the student's previous practical-training history. The student should have the DSO review every prior OPT period, SEVIS record, and EAD at that educational level.
- **A full 12 months of master's-level OPT already used:** A second master's generally does not provide a new post-completion OPT period at the same educational level. Moving to a higher educational level, such as a doctorate, may create a new 12-month practical-training allotment, subject to eligibility.
- **Full-time CPT history:** One year or more of full-time CPT at the same educational level can make the student ineligible for post-completion OPT at that level, even if they did not use post-completion OPT after the first master's.

These are general rules, not an individual eligibility decision. “Second master's” can describe different school, degree, transfer, and prior-status histories. The DSO must assess the student's full record and enter the recommendation in SEVIS; USCIS adjudicates the Form I-765.

## STEM OPT questions

A separate 24-month STEM OPT extension is not guaranteed by earning a second master's. A qualifying STEM degree, current post-completion OPT, eligible E-Verify employer, and the required Form I-983 and filing steps are needed. Regulations permit a later STEM OPT extension only within the lifetime limit and, for a second extension, it must be based on a degree at a higher level than the degree used for the first extension. Some students may be able to rely on a previously earned STEM degree for an extension, subject to detailed requirements; ask the DSO to review the degree, CIP code, timing, employer, and prior STEM OPT history.

## Changes that can affect current OPT

Starting a new degree program, transferring schools, or changing education level can affect or automatically terminate existing OPT authorization. Do not enroll, transfer, travel, or start work based on assumptions about how a pending or approved OPT period will be treated. Ask the current and new school's DSOs to confirm the exact SEVIS release date and effect on work authorization before acting.

## Questions for the DSO

Bring the DSO copies/details for all prior Forms I-20, SEVIS IDs, CPT authorizations, OPT EADs, dates used, prior degrees, and any STEM OPT extensions. Ask:

1. How many months of post-completion OPT have I used at the master's level?
2. Does my full-time CPT history at this level affect remaining eligibility?
3. Does my second program count at the same educational level, and how will a transfer or new program affect my current status or work authorization?
4. If I seek STEM OPT, which degree/CIP code would qualify and have I used any prior STEM extension?
5. What are the current USCIS filing windows, fees, and required documents for my particular application?
