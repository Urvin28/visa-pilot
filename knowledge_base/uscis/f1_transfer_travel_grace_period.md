# F-1 transfers, travel, and grace periods

These topics depend on the student's SEVIS record, program dates, visa, and travel history. Coordinate with the DSO before changing schools, leaving the United States, or making plans near the end of a program or OPT period.

## Transfer to another school

An F-1 student may generally transfer an active SEVIS record to another SEVP-certified school. The student should first discuss the plan with the current school's DSO, obtain admission and a transfer Form I-20 from the new school, and agree with the DSOs on a SEVIS release date. The new school must complete the required SEVIS registration and the student must begin attendance by the applicable next session/start date. A transfer during OPT can end OPT authorization, so do not transfer or change programs without confirming the effect with both DSOs.

## Changing education level or extending a program

Students moving to a new educational level or beginning a new program need a new Form I-20 and timely SEVIS updates. If more time is needed to complete the current program, contact the DSO before the program end date; a program extension generally must be requested before that date and requires an academic reason and updated funding information. Do not wait until the I-20 end date has passed.

## International travel and return

Before departing, ask the DSO to confirm that the SEVIS record is in the right status and that the Form I-20 has a valid travel endorsement. For reentry, travelers commonly need a valid passport, a valid F-1 visa unless visa-exempt, and the endorsed Form I-20, plus any documents relevant to their current activity (such as an EAD and current employment evidence during OPT). A visa allows a person to seek entry; U.S. Customs and Border Protection decides admission at the port of entry. A visa expiration while inside the United States does not by itself mean the person must immediately leave, but a new visa is generally needed to return after international travel if no exception applies.

Travel while an OPT or STEM OPT application is pending, after a status violation, with an expired visa, or near a program transition can carry additional risk. Get individualized guidance before booking or departing.

## 60-day grace period

Under the longstanding F-1 framework, students who successfully complete their program, and students whose authorized post-completion OPT ends, generally have a 60-day grace period to prepare to depart, transfer, or take another permitted step. The grace period is not employment authorization. It does not generally apply after a status violation or an early withdrawal/termination; contact the DSO immediately if studies end unexpectedly.

The DHS 2026 rule that proposed fixed admission dates was vacated by a federal district court on November 16, 2026. Its proposed stay and grace-period changes should not be presented as current law without checking for new court or agency action.
