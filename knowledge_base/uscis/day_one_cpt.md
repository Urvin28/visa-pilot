# Day 1 CPT: what the term means and what the rules require

“Day 1 CPT” is an informal phrase, not a separate visa category or a special kind of work permit. It usually describes a school-authorized Curricular Practical Training (CPT) placement that begins during the first academic year of a graduate program. Whether a particular student and job qualify depends on the program, curriculum, student history, and DSO authorization—not the program's marketing label.

## When CPT may begin during the first academic year

The general rule requires an F-1 student to complete one full academic year of lawful full-time study at an approved SEVP-certified college, university, conservatory, or seminary before practical training. Federal regulation provides an exception for students enrolled in graduate studies that require immediate participation in CPT. This is a narrow, program-specific exception: a student should not assume that any graduate program, internship option, or school's Day 1 CPT policy qualifies.

Before relying on the exception, ask the DSO to explain in writing how the internship or practical training is required by, and integrated into, the established curriculum for the student's specific degree program and why the program qualifies for the graduate immediate-participation exception. A DSO's ability to enter CPT in SEVIS does not make every underlying arrangement legally eligible, and an employer's offer alone does not authorize work.

## CPT requirements that still apply

- The training must be directly related to the student's major area of study and an integral part of the school's established curriculum.
- The student must have a specific training opportunity before CPT can be authorized. The authorization is for the named employer, location, dates, and full-time or part-time basis.
- The DSO must authorize the CPT in SEVIS and issue a Form I-20 showing the authorization. The student may begin only on or after the authorized start date shown on the CPT I-20. CPT cannot be backdated.
- The student must continue to meet F-1 study and status requirements. CPT is not a general employment benefit and is not interchangeable with OPT.
- One year or more of full-time CPT makes a student ineligible for post-completion OPT at the same educational level. The student's cumulative CPT history matters, including authorization from prior schools at that same level.

## Questions to ask the school before enrolling or accepting a job

1. Is practical training required by the curriculum for this exact degree and cohort, or is it optional employment marketed as curricular?
2. Which specific course, credit, learning objective, or degree requirement makes this training integral to the curriculum?
3. Does the program require immediate CPT participation under the graduate-study exception, and what documentation supports that conclusion?
4. What enrollment, attendance, academic progress, and in-person requirements apply each term? Can the student realistically satisfy them while working?
5. What exact employer, worksite, duties, hours, and dates will appear in SEVIS and on the Form I-20? When may work legally start?
6. How will full-time or part-time CPT be calculated, and how much full-time CPT has already been used at this degree level?
7. What are the consequences for future OPT eligibility, school transfer, travel, or a later change in education level?

Get case-specific advice from the DSO before beginning work. If a program's explanation is unclear, or the student is deciding whether to enroll, an experienced immigration attorney can review the curriculum and individual circumstances. Do not begin employment while waiting for authorization.

## Short answer for common questions

**Can F-1 students do Day 1 CPT?** Sometimes a graduate student may qualify for CPT before completing one academic year if the graduate program requires immediate CPT participation and all CPT requirements are met. The phrase “Day 1 CPT” by itself does not establish eligibility. The DSO must authorize the specific training in SEVIS, and the student must have the endorsed CPT Form I-20 before working.

**Is Day 1 CPT automatically illegal?** The label alone does not decide legality. The controlling questions are whether the graduate-study exception applies, the training is genuinely integral to the established curriculum and directly related to the major, and the student and school meet all applicable requirements.

**Can I start after getting a job offer or after asking the DSO?** No. Do not begin until the DSO has authorized the specific CPT in SEVIS, issued the endorsed Form I-20, and the CPT start date has arrived.

**Does CPT affect OPT?** One year or more of full-time CPT makes the student ineligible for post-completion OPT at that educational level. Part-time CPT does not trigger that specific bar, but students should have their full CPT history reviewed by the DSO.
