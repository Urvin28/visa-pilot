# F-1 work and Curricular Practical Training (CPT)

F-1 employment is limited and category-specific. Always get written guidance from your DSO before starting a job, internship, gig, volunteer role that may be employment, or other work. An employer's willingness to hire you does not establish immigration work authorization.

## On-campus employment

Eligible F-1 students may generally work on campus subject to the regulatory conditions. While school is in session, the general limit is 20 hours per week; full-time on-campus work may be allowed during official school breaks when the student is eligible and intends to register for the next term. The DSO can confirm whether a particular job and schedule qualify as on-campus employment.

## CPT

Curricular Practical Training is employment or training that is an integral part of an established curriculum, such as a required internship, cooperative education placement, or practicum. It is authorized by the DSO in SEVIS for a specific employer, location, dates, and full-time or part-time basis. The student must receive an updated Form I-20 showing the CPT authorization before beginning work. CPT is not a general work permit and cannot be used simply because a student has found an internship.

Students generally must have completed one full academic year in valid F-1 status before CPT, except for graduate programs that require immediate participation in CPT. School and program requirements also apply. Full-time CPT totaling 12 months or more at the same educational level makes a student ineligible for post-completion OPT at that level. Part-time CPT does not trigger that particular 12-month bar.

“Day 1 CPT” is an informal label for CPT at the start of a program; it is not its own authorization category. The graduate-program exception is limited to graduate studies that require immediate CPT participation. A program's advertising or ability to enter an authorization in SEVIS does not alone establish that the student qualifies. See [Day 1 CPT: what the term means and what the rules require](day_one_cpt.md).

## Other off-campus work categories

- **OPT:** Work directly related to the student's major, authorized by USCIS after a DSO recommendation and Form I-765 approval/EAD (except limited automatic extensions under specific rules). See the OPT guide.
- **STEM OPT:** A 24-month extension for qualifying students with an eligible STEM degree and a qualifying E-Verify employer; additional reporting and training-plan rules apply.
- **Severe economic hardship:** Some students may apply to USCIS for off-campus employment authorization after meeting eligibility requirements, including one academic year in F-1 status. Do not work until authorized and the EAD is effective.
- **International organization employment:** A narrow authorization category for qualifying organizations, requiring DSO and USCIS steps.

## Before accepting a role

Ask your DSO whether the work fits an authorized category, whether a new Form I-20 or USCIS approval is needed, what the exact start date is, and what hours/employer/location limits apply. Do not start early while an application is pending. Keep authorization documents and records of employment. Contact the DSO if the employer, worksite, hours, duties, or dates change.
