# F-1 student status: practical basics

This guide is general information for students in F-1 academic programs. A student's DSO and the current government rules are the authoritative sources for an individual case. Immigration rules and agency procedures can change; confirm time-sensitive decisions with the DSO and USCIS.

## Before arrival and starting school

- Attend an SEVP-certified school and use the Form I-20 issued for the school and program you will attend.
- Pay the I-901 SEVIS fee when required, obtain the appropriate visa if required, and bring your passport, visa, Form I-20, and supporting documents when traveling.
- Initial F-1 students generally may enter no earlier than 30 days before the program start date on the Form I-20.
- Report to the school and DSO by the program start date and follow the school's check-in instructions. If plans change or you cannot arrive, contact the DSO before the start date.

## Maintaining status during study

- Enroll in and make normal progress toward the required full course of study for your school and program. The definition depends on the program and school type.
- Do not drop below a full course load, stop attending, take a leave, or change program dates without speaking with the DSO first. A DSO may authorize a reduced course load only for qualifying reasons and generally must enter the authorization in SEVIS before the student reduces the load.
- Keep the passport valid, keep the Form I-20 and SEVIS information accurate, and report address changes to the DSO promptly (the federal rule generally requires notice within 10 days).
- Work only when the specific F-1 employment category and authorization allow it. An offer letter by itself does not authorize work.
- Ask the DSO promptly about academic difficulty, medical leave, program extensions, school changes, travel, or an error in the SEVIS record.

## Duration of status and current rule update

F-1 students traditionally have been admitted for “duration of status” (D/S), meaning they may remain while pursuing the authorized program and any applicable authorized practical training and grace period, subject to the rules. DHS published a 2026 rule that would replace D/S with fixed admission dates. A federal district court entered a preliminary injunction postponing that rule and, on November 16, 2026, vacated it on summary judgment. As of this guide's review date, do not assume the fixed-date rule is in effect; check current court and DHS/USCIS updates because further proceedings or appeals may affect the legal situation.

## If something goes wrong

Falling out of status can have serious consequences. Do not rely on an online checklist to decide whether to depart, apply for reinstatement, or continue working. Contact the DSO promptly and consider advice from a qualified immigration attorney. USCIS uses Form I-539 for eligible F-1 reinstatement requests; filing does not itself guarantee approval or employment authorization.
